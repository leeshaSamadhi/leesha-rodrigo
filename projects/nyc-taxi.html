01 / The business question

Where should planners look closer?

A city-wide average can hide large differences in taxi prices and demand. The task was to compare pricing across pickup zones and times, then identify locations where pickups and drop-offs are out of balance.

Two decisions to support

Where should pricing reliability be investigated? And where could taxi staging and dispatch better reflect demand?

My proposed solution was a repeatable analysis that cleans the trip data, joins it to taxi-zone names and produces simple comparisons of fares, variability and demand. It supports further investigation, rather than claiming to prove unfair pricing or an actual shortage of taxis.

02 / From raw trips to useful comparisons

Clean first. Then ask better questions.

I loaded the 12 monthly Parquet files in Databricks and joined the taxi-zone lookup. Cleaning removed 3,015,113 records (7.60%) before calculating fares per mile and demand patterns.

12 monthly files → Databricks / PySpark → 39,656,098 trip records. Inspect missing values, non-positive fares, implausible distances and inconsistent timestamps.
See the cleaning rules and measurement choices
  • Keep distances above 0.1 and up to 250 miles, and fare amounts above $0 and below $300.
  • Require non-null pickup and drop-off zone IDs and a recorded, non-zero passenger count.
  • Require drop-off after pickup and a trip duration of no more than six hours.
  • Use fare amount ÷ distance. This is the metered fare per mile, not the full passenger payment including tips, tolls and extra charges.
  • Use the median (the middle trip’s value) and the middle 50% range to describe typical prices and variation.
  • For zone pricing comparisons, show NYC zones with at least 1,000 retained trips. The chart covers 140 eligible zones.

04 / Place and predictability

Some zones have a much wider spread.

South Ozone Park’s typical fare was $7.76 per mile, with a middle-50% spread of $19.56. At JFK Airport, the corresponding figures were $2.89 and $0.34. Compare any two eligible zones below.

Gold band = the middle 50% of fares (25th–75th percentile). Dark marker = the median. Both bands use the same scale, starting at zero. IQR is the width of the gold band.

Show the numbers

Planning implication: prioritise zones with wide price variation for a closer review. A wide spread means less predictable observed fares; it does not, by itself, establish unfair treatment.

07 / My contribution and learning

From technical checks to a clear decision story.

  • Prepared the data: profiled quality issues and applied documented filters to the full year of trip records.
  • Built the analysis: used PySpark joins, grouped counts, approximate percentiles and time segmentation to compare prices and demand.
  • Communicated findings: paired charts with practical recommendations and separated observed patterns from possible explanations.

I learned to pair a typical value with its spread, test whether small samples distort rankings, and explain what a metric cannot tell us as clearly as what it can.

About the numbers in this portfolio

The interactive charts were verified from the 12 supplied Parquet files using the notebook’s cleaning rules and the supplied taxi-zone lookup. Counts match the notebook: 39,656,098 before cleaning and 36,640,985 after. Percentiles here are recalculated exactly; the original PySpark analysis used approximate percentiles, so small differences are expected.

The separately supplied zone-summary CSV exports do not match this filtered run and are not mixed into these charts. The analysis uses the monthly files as supplied without adding a separate pickup-year filter. Only aggregated statistics are published; individual trip records are not included.

08 / Three intended impacts

Evidence that points to the next action.

Investigate pricing reliability

Target follow-up work in high-variation zones, including South Ozone Park, Richmond Hill, Randalls Island and Kew Gardens.

Review airport taxi positioning

Use the pickup–drop-off gaps at JFK and LaGuardia to assess whether staging and dispatch match demand.

Plan by time of day

Use morning and evening demand patterns to guide taxi allocation and identify opportunities to reduce unnecessary repositioning.

These are proposed benefits. Changes in waiting time, operating costs or pricing equity were not measured.
